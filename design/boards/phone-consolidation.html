Lupe phone consolidation: iPhone vs Android

One row per component, iPhone left, Android right. Pick a keeper for every row marked open. Rows marked decided carry Scott's earlier call and stay for reference.

iPhone: simulator iPhone 17 Pro, Skiff demo, firmware 2.5.1, demo link. Android: the latest p2-shell debug build (app-debug.apk, 14 Nov 17:08) on the fake core, scenario "ready". The Pixel 7 was locked behind its fingerprint/PIN, so the Android screens come from the same APK on a Pixel-sized emulator (1080x2400, 420 dpi). Same pixels and density as the Pixel 7, but not the physical phone. Captured 14 Nov 2026.

1 · Pads page, full screen

open

iPhone always draws eight lettered columns A to H and puts a ⌄⌄ tab under the selected pad. Android draws only filled slots and shows ×N pass counts and a "next" cue on the pads.

2 · Track heads

decidedKeeper: Android

iPhone shows a number key and a speaker icon for mute. Android shows a colour block with the number, the track name and engine, and a MUTE word button.

3 · Selected track

decidedKeeper: iPhone

iPhone tints the whole row in the track's muted colour and rings the number key. Android draws a track-coloured outline round the row.

4 · Header: tabs, transport, tempo, launch timing

decidedKeeper: Android, with tabs Pads · Edit · Timeline

iPhone: track chip, four boxed tabs (Pads, Edit, Mix, Sounds), a Bar key, rec, stop, position over BPM, and ⋯. Android: a song-name menu, three underlined tabs, Bar ⌄, position over tempo, and round rec and play buttons.

5 · Scenes

open

On both, scenes run as a row of 16 under the pads, not a column. iPhone has a short "Store" key with a dot and outlined empty scenes. Android has a wider "Store scene" button and draws empty scenes as plain numbers.

6 · Edit page

decidedKeeper: iPhone step grid plus a small piano down its left edge

iPhone shows a piano-roll grid with note names down the left. Android shows one strip of 16 step cells, then a playable keyboard, Chord and Lane, and four sound knobs.

7 · Sound page pop-up on Pads

open

iPhone's panel covers the right two-thirds, with a title, page tabs, the track's sound card and four value tiles. Android's tinted card sits bottom-right, shows "FX 1/7" with four dials over four sliders, and has no page tabs.

The iPhone rail is off by default (Settings › Sound buttons). It was switched on only for this capture, then switched off again. With it off, the panel opens from the FM-1's own keys.

8 · Hold menus

decidedKeeper: Android styling with the iPhone/iPad's full option set

iPhone opens a two-column grid of large tinted buttons (10 on a pad, 4 on a track). Android opens a callout list with leader lines from a touch disc (4 on a pad, 3 on a track).

9 · Mix / GLO

decidedKeeper: a pop-up with no title, tabs inline with the close button

On iPhone, Mix is a tab, with sections down the left and level, pan and reverb on every row. On Android it's a titled sheet with sections across the top and a two-column mixer (level and send only). Global differs too: BPM and swing tiles on iPhone; tempo, swing, master and transpose dials on Android.

10 · Sounds and Projects

open

On iPhone, Sounds is a tab (engine list, presets in mixed case) and there is no Projects screen. On Android, Sounds is a sheet opened from a track's hold menu (uppercase presets, Save and Done), and Projects is a sheet with the FM-1's four slots.

11 · Timeline

open

iPhone has no timeline. Android's Timeline tab shows one lane per track, bars across the top, zoom buttons, and a "Stop to change the song" lock while playing.

12 · Settings

open

iPhone opens a full-screen sheet with a checkmark list and a Done button, plus handedness. Android opens a bottom sheet with a close button and grouped sections (FM-1, theme, slide to turn as a segmented control, Magnifier). The two apps also hold different option sets.

13 · Other differences

open

13a · The app menu (⋯ vs the song name)

iPhone's ⋯ menu lists play through the iPhone, the source (USB, Skiff demo, SLOOP demo), Settings and Developer. Android's song-name menu lists Projects, Mix, Settings and Labs, plus the fake core's FM-1 keys.

13b · Launch timing menu

Both offer the same four choices (Loop, Bar, Beat, Now). iPhone adds a "Nothing waiting / Cancel waiting" footer and opens from the top edge; Android hangs a smaller menu under Bar ⌄.

13c · No-FM-1 state

iPhone keeps the header and shows a card ("Plug in the FM-1"), the firmware versions and a "Use the demo FM-1" button. Android shows only centred text and a progress bar, with no header and no demo button.

The iPhone card names Skiff 2.2, not 2.5.1. The Android status bar shows only because the emulator's System UI had restarted; on the Pixel it stays hidden.

13d · Track controls box (TrackKnobBox)

iPhone doesn't show the box on phones (PadsPage turns it off for the phone layout; it hangs from the selected pad on iPad). Android's nearest match is the four sound knobs on the Edit page.
